Constraints

The residual and the tangent

A constraint of EdelweissMeshfree is an EdelweissFE constraint, and enters the equation system by EdelweissFE’s rule (see the EdelweissFE solver documentation, The residual and the tangent): its computeKernels(U_np, dU, P, K, timeStep) adds the residual contribution \(\mathbf{P}\) – the negative of the force the constraint exerts on its nodes, what an element of the same mechanics would add as its internal force – and its tangent \(\mathbf{K} = \partial \mathbf{P} / \partial \mathbf{U}\). A penalty constraint that pulls the displacement \(u = \mathbf{N} \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U}\) of a particle towards \(\bar{u}\) adds

\[\mathbf{P} \mathrel{+}= k\, \mathbf{N}\, (\mathbf{N} \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U} - \bar{u}), \qquad \mathbf{K} \mathrel{+}= k\, \mathbf{N} \otimes \mathbf{N} .\]

The nodal values of the particles and of the cells are increments on the current configuration, so these constraints read the increment dU. Their nodes are those the particles or material points currently interpolate from: a constraint finds them anew at the start of every increment, in updateConnectivity, which may also find the constraint out of reach (withinReach), e.g. a contact constraint whose particle is far from the boundary. A constraint takes part in the equation system (active) while it is within reach, unless it is switched off from outside: setting active to False, e.g. by a modelupdate step action, switches a constraint off whatever it finds in reach, until it is switched on again. Switched off from outside, it rests: the model does not locate it anew, and it keeps what it found and computed last, its reaction force included. These are the rules of EdelweissFE’s constraint base class, see the EdelweissFE constraint documentation.

Because \(\mathbf{K}\) is the derivative of exactly what is added to \(\mathbf{P}\), each constraint is checked by central differences of its own computeKernels in edelweissmeshfree/constraints/test/residual_tangent_test.py, with EdelweissFE’s check (edelweissfe.utils.residualtangentcheck); add a case there for a new one.

A constraint written for explicit analyses alone, with forces and without a tangent, derives from MPMExplicitConstraintBase and implements computeKernelsExplicit(U_np, dU, P, timeStep), with the same sign: an implicit solver refuses it.

The base classes

Constraints are used to enforce certain conditions on the system. They are used to enforce boundary conditions, contact conditions, etc. Technically, a constraint is an entity of the equation system like an element: it adds a residual contribution and its tangent, and it may introduce additional scalar variables (e.g., Lagrange multipliers) to enforce its condition. Unlike elements, constraints are not subject to external loads.

class edelweissmeshfree.constraints.base.mpmconstraintbase.MPMConstraintBase(name, model, *args, **kwargs)[source]

The base class of the constraints of EdelweissMeshfree; implement a new constraint by deriving from it.

A meshfree constraint is an EdelweissFE constraint (ConstraintBase), and follows its rules:

  • computeKernels() (U_np, dU, P, K, timeStep) adds the constraint’s residual contribution to P – the negative of the force it exerts on its nodes, what an element of the same mechanics would add as its internal force – and its tangent K = dP/dU. See the EdelweissFE solver documentation, The residual and the tangent.

  • The nodes of a meshfree constraint are those its particles or material points currently interpolate from, so they change as the kernel functions and cells move: updateConnectivity finds them anew at the start of every increment, and reports a change.

  • A constraint may switch itself off in updateConnectivity, e.g. a contact constraint whose particle is far from the boundary: it sets withinReach, and reports a change of active. A constraint switched off from outside, through active, stays off whatever it finds, and rests: the model does not locate it anew.

  • Every constraint declares the state it carries from one increment to the next, in checkpointedState (empty if it carries none), so that a restart can restore it.

The nodal values of the particles and of the cells are increments on the current configuration, so the constraints here read dU, the increment since the last converged state, and leave U_np unused.

A constraint that acts only in an explicit analysis, with forces and without a tangent, derives from MPMExplicitConstraintBase instead.

computeKernels(U_np, dU, P, K, timeStep)[source]

Add this constraint’s contribution P to the residual, and its tangent K = dP/dU.

A constraint follows the rule every element and every load follows (see The residual and the tangent): P is the force the constraint needs at its degrees of freedom, i.e. the negative of the force it exerts on the nodes, exactly as an element of the same mechanics would add its internal force. A penalty spring of stiffness \(k\) that pulls a degree of freedom \(u\) towards \(\bar{u}\), for example, adds

\[P \mathrel{+}= k\,(u - \bar{u}), \qquad K \mathrel{+}= k .\]

A Lagrange multiplier constraint \(g(\boldsymbol{u}) = 0\) adds \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^T \partial g / \partial \boldsymbol{u}\) to the rows of its nodes and \(g\) to the rows of its multipliers.

Both P and K are augmented, never overwritten. K must be the derivative of what is added to P: a test can then check it by finite differences, which also catches a P whose sign alone is wrong. A P and a K negated together pass that check, so a test checks the direction of the forces the constraint exerts as well.

Parameters:

  • U_np (ndarray) – The current solution, restricted to this constraint’s degrees of freedom; its own scalar variables (e.g. Lagrange multipliers) come last.

  • dU (ndarray) – The solution increment since the last converged state, likewise restricted.

  • P (ndarray) – The residual contribution to augment.

  • K (ndarray) – The tangent dP/dU to augment, in the shape shapeVIJContribution() gives it (a dense nDof x nDof view unless the constraint declares a sparser pattern). A constraint that inherits ForcesOnlyExplicitEvaluation is also called with K=None and must then skip the tangent.

  • timeStep (TimeStep) – The current time step.

A penalty spring of stiffness \(k\) that pulls a degree of freedom \(u\) towards \(\bar{u}\), for example, adds

\[P \mathrel{+}= k\,(u - \bar{u}), \qquad K \mathrel{+}= k .\]

A Lagrange multiplier constraint \(g(\boldsymbol{u}) = 0\) adds \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^T \partial g / \partial \boldsymbol{u}\) to the rows of its nodes and \(g\) to the rows of its multipliers.

A P and a K negated together pass that check, so a test checks the direction of the forces the constraint exerts as well.

Prescribes the increment of a field at one point of one particle – its center, the center of one of its faces, or one of its vertices – with Lagrange multipliers.

It is the Lagrange multiplier counterpart of ParticlePenaltyWeakDirichlet. A penalty spring enforces the prescribed value only approximately, the more closely the stiffer it is; a multiplier enforces it exactly, at the cost of one more unknown per prescribed component, and of a zero block on the diagonal of the tangent. A particle interpolates the field from the nodes of its kernel functions, as an increment on the current configuration: \(u(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{N}(\boldsymbol{x}) \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U}\). At the constrained point \(\boldsymbol{x}_c\) and for every prescribed component \(i\), a multiplier \(\lambda_i\) enforces

\[g_i = \boldsymbol{N}(\boldsymbol{x}_c) \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U}_i - \Delta\bar{u}_i = 0,\]

computeKernels(U_np, dU, P, K, timeStep)[source]

Add this constraint’s contribution P to the residual, and its tangent K = dP/dU.

A constraint follows the rule every element and every load follows (see The residual and the tangent): P is the force the constraint needs at its degrees of freedom, i.e. the negative of the force it exerts on the nodes, exactly as an element of the same mechanics would add its internal force. A penalty spring of stiffness \(k\) that pulls a degree of freedom \(u\) towards \(\bar{u}\), for example, adds

\[P \mathrel{+}= k\,(u - \bar{u}), \qquad K \mathrel{+}= k .\]

A Lagrange multiplier constraint \(g(\boldsymbol{u}) = 0\) adds \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^T \partial g / \partial \boldsymbol{u}\) to the rows of its nodes and \(g\) to the rows of its multipliers.

Both P and K are augmented, never overwritten. K must be the derivative of what is added to P: a test can then check it by finite differences, which also catches a P whose sign alone is wrong. A P and a K negated together pass that check, so a test checks the direction of the forces the constraint exerts as well.

Parameters:

  • U_np (ndarray) – The current solution, restricted to this constraint’s degrees of freedom; its own scalar variables (e.g. Lagrange multipliers) come last.

  • dU (ndarray) – The solution increment since the last converged state, likewise restricted.

  • P (ndarray) – The residual contribution to augment.

  • K (ndarray) – The tangent dP/dU to augment, in the shape shapeVIJContribution() gives it (a dense nDof x nDof view unless the constraint declares a sparser pattern). A constraint that inherits ForcesOnlyExplicitEvaluation is also called with K=None and must then skip the tangent.

  • timeStep (TimeStep) – The current time step.

This class implements a Penalty contact boundary constraint for EXPLICIT simulations. It prevents a particle from penetrating a defined boundary surface by applying penalty forces directly to the grid nodes.

The constraint is applied based on the current position of the particle relative to the boundary. If penetration is detected, the penalty force is

\[\boldsymbol{F}_{\mathrm{penalty}} = -k\, g\, \boldsymbol{n},\]
