Constraints

The residual and the tangent

A constraint of EdelweissMeshfree is an EdelweissFE constraint, and enters the discrete system by EdelweissFE’s rule (see the EdelweissFE solver documentation, The residual and the tangent): its computeKernels(U_np, dU, P, K, timeStep) adds the residual contribution \(\mathbf{P}\) – the negative of the force the constraint exerts on its nodes, what an element of the same mechanics would add as its internal force – and its tangent \(\mathbf{K} = \partial \mathbf{P} / \partial \mathbf{U}\). A penalty constraint that pulls the displacement \(u = \mathbf{N} \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U}\) of a particle towards \(\bar{u}\) adds

\[\mathbf{P} \mathrel{+}= k\, \mathbf{N}\, (\mathbf{N} \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U} - \bar{u}), \qquad \mathbf{K} \mathrel{+}= k\, \mathbf{N} \otimes \mathbf{N} .\]

The nodal values of the particles and of the cells are increments on the current configuration, so these constraints read the increment dU. Their nodes are those the particles or material points currently interpolate from: a constraint finds them anew at the start of every increment, in updateConnectivity, which may also find the constraint out of reach (withinReach), e.g. a contact constraint whose particle is far from the boundary. A constraint takes part in the discrete system (active) while it is within reach, unless it is switched off from outside: setting active to False, e.g. by a modelupdate step action, switches a constraint off whatever it finds in reach, until it is switched on again. Switched off from outside, it rests: the model does not locate it anew, and it keeps what it found and computed last, its reaction force included. These are the rules of EdelweissFE’s constraint base class, see the EdelweissFE constraint documentation.

A constraint with Lagrange multipliers adds them as scalar variables of the model. The condition \(g\) it enforces is one on increments, like the nodal values; the multiplier \(\lambda\) is the force or traction that balances the total stresses of the particles or elements, so it is a total. A multiplier that prescribes the increment \(\Delta\bar{u}\) of a particle’s displacement \(u = \mathbf{N} \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U}\) adds

\[\mathbf{P} \mathrel{+}= \lambda\, \mathbf{N}, \qquad P_{\lambda} \mathrel{+}= \mathbf{N} \cdot \Delta\mathbf{U} - \Delta\bar{u}, \qquad \mathbf{K}_{U\lambda} \mathrel{+}= \mathbf{N}, \qquad \mathbf{K}_{\lambda U} \mathrel{+}= \mathbf{N}^T,\]

with \(\lambda\) read from U_np: the solver continues it from the last converged increment, also across a rebuilt discrete system, as the model keeps the converged multipliers in its scalar variables, and its checkpoint keeps them for a restart. The residual is linear in \(\lambda\), so where Newton’s method starts \(\lambda\) changes only its first residual, not the iterates after the first correction. The deprecated solvers (edelweissmeshfree.solvers.deprecated) continue the multipliers only while the discrete system is kept, restart them at zero after a rebuild, and do not check the residual of the multipliers for convergence.

Because \(\mathbf{K}\) is the derivative of exactly what is added to \(\mathbf{P}\), each constraint is checked by central differences of its own computeKernels in edelweissmeshfree/constraints/test/residual_tangent_test.py, with EdelweissFE’s check (edelweissfe.utils.residualtangentcheck); add a case there for a new one.

A constraint written for explicit analyses alone, with forces and without a tangent, derives from MPMExplicitConstraintBase and implements computeKernelsExplicit(U_np, dU, P, timeStep), with the same sign: an implicit solver refuses it. The explicit solver, in turn, accepts only a constraint that acts in an explicit analysis (actsInExplicitAnalysis): one that measures its condition at the particles’ current positions, as the penalty contacts do, explicit or not. A constraint on the increment, such as a weak Dirichlet condition, would act as if nothing had moved there.

The base classes

Constraints are used to enforce certain conditions on the system. They are used to enforce boundary conditions, contact conditions, etc. Technically, a constraint is an entity of the discrete system like an element: it adds a residual contribution and its tangent, and it may introduce additional scalar variables (e.g., Lagrange multipliers) to enforce its condition. Unlike elements, constraints are not subject to external loads.

class edelweissmeshfree.constraints.base.mpmconstraintbase.MPMConstraintBase(name, model, *args, **kwargs)[source]

The base class of the constraints of EdelweissMeshfree; implement a new constraint by deriving from it.

A meshfree constraint is an EdelweissFE constraint (ConstraintBase), and follows its rules:

  • computeKernels() (U_np, dU, P, K, timeStep) adds the constraint’s residual contribution to P – the negative of the force it exerts on its nodes, what an element of the same mechanics would add as its internal force – and its tangent K = dP/dU. See the EdelweissFE solver documentation, The residual and the tangent.

  • The nodes of a meshfree constraint are those its particles or material points currently interpolate from, so they change as the kernel functions and cells move: updateConnectivity finds them anew at the start of every increment, and reports whether they changed.

  • A constraint may switch itself off in updateConnectivity, e.g. a contact constraint whose particle is far from the boundary: it sets withinReach; the model sees the change of active itself. A constraint switched off from outside, through active, stays off whatever it finds, and rests: the model does not locate it anew.

  • Every constraint declares the state it carries from one increment to the next, in checkpointedState (empty if it carries none), so that a restart can restore it.

The nodal values of the particles and of the cells are increments on the current configuration, so the constraints here read them from dU, the increment since the last converged state. A Lagrange multiplier, in contrast, is a force or a traction, which balances the total stresses of the particles or elements: it is a total value, read from U_np, where the solver continues it from the last converged increment. The model holds the multipliers as its scalar variables, and its checkpoint holds them with it.

A constraint that acts only in an explicit analysis, with forces and without a tangent, derives from MPMExplicitConstraintBase instead. Whether a constraint acts in an explicit analysis at all, it declares in actsInExplicitAnalysis.

An explicit analysis solves no linear system, so it needs a constraint’s forces but not their tangent. computeKernels(U_np, dU, P, K, timeStep)[source]

Add this constraint’s contribution P to the residual, and its tangent K = dP/dU.

A constraint follows the rule every element and every load follows (see The residual and the tangent): P is the force the constraint needs at its degrees of freedom, i.e. the negative of the force it exerts on the nodes, exactly as an element of the same mechanics would add its internal force. A penalty spring of stiffness \(k\) that pulls a degree of freedom \(u\) towards \(\bar{u}\), for example, adds

\[P \mathrel{+}= k\,(u - \bar{u}), \qquad K \mathrel{+}= k .\]

A Lagrange multiplier constraint \(g(\boldsymbol{u}) = 0\) adds \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^T \partial g / \partial \boldsymbol{u}\) to the rows of its nodes and \(g\) to the rows of its multipliers.

Both P and K are augmented, never overwritten. K must be the derivative of what is added to P: a test can then check it by finite differences, which also catches a P whose sign alone is wrong. A P and a K negated together pass that check, so a test checks the direction of the forces the constraint exerts as well.

Parameters:

  • U_np (ndarray) – The current solution, restricted to this constraint’s degrees of freedom; its own scalar variables (e.g. Lagrange multipliers) come last.

  • dU (ndarray) – The solution increment since the last converged state, likewise restricted.

  • P (ndarray) – The residual contribution to augment.

  • K (ndarray) – The tangent dP/dU to augment, in the shape shapeBlock() gives it (a dense nDof x nDof view unless the constraint declares a sparser pattern). A constraint that inherits ForcesOnlyExplicitEvaluation is also called with K=None and must then skip the tangent.

  • timeStep (TimeStep) – The current time step.

Mortar-type weak Dirichlet boundary condition on a set of particles.

Instead of collocating the constraint pointwise at every particle (which produces alternating point reactions on nearly incompressible media and, with it, a pressure checkerboard along the boundary), the constraint is enforced in the integrated sense

g_k = sum_q w_q P_k(xi_q) ( u_i(x_q) - ubar_i ) = 0, k = 0..multiplierOrder,

class edelweissmeshfree.constraints.particlefiniteelementmortartie.ParticleFiniteElementMortarTie(name, model, interfaceParticles, finiteElementInterfaceNodes, quadraturePointsPerEdge=8)[source]

Ties an RKPM domain to a finite element domain along a common interface, in two dimensions: the displacement of the particles and the displacement of the finite elements agree on the interface in the weak (mortar) sense.

What is tied. On the interface \(\Gamma\), the finite element side interpolates the displacement with the shape functions \(N_a\) of its quadratic element edges, the RKPM side with the reproducing kernel shape functions \(\Psi_K\) of the particles there. The two do not match pointwise: the reproducing kernel shape functions are smooth and not interpolating, the element edges are piecewise polynomials. The tie therefore asks that their difference be orthogonal to the multiplier space – the shape functions of the element edges, one multiplier per interface node and displacement component:

\[g_{ai} = \int_\Gamma N_a \left( \sum_b N_b\, \Delta U_{bi} - \sum_K \Psi_K\, \Delta U_{Ki} \right) d\Gamma = 0.\]

Incremental, on the current configuration. RKPM is updated-Lagrangian: its nodal values are the coefficients of one increment, on the configuration at the start of the increment, where the kernel functions sit with their particles. The tie therefore couples increments \(\Delta U\), and integrates on that configuration: the element edges are placed at their nodes’ converged positions, \(\boldsymbol{x} = \boldsymbol{X} + \boldsymbol{U}\). Both sides start stress-free and tied, so the increments add up to a tied total displacement. During an increment the configuration is fixed, so \(g\) is linear in \(\Delta U\), and its derivatives

\[\frac{\partial g_{ai}}{\partial \Delta U_{bi}} = \int_\Gamma N_a N_b \, d\Gamma, \qquad \frac{\partial g_{ai}}{\partial \Delta U_{Ki}} = -\int_\Gamma N_a \Psi_K \, d\Gamma\]

are computed once per increment, in updateConnectivity().

Forces. The multiplier \(\lambda_{ai}\) is the interface traction, interpolated like the displacement of the element edges. Unlike the increments it couples, it is a total quantity: the stresses on both sides are total, so is the force that the interface transmits between them. Following the rule of every constraint (see MPMConstraintBase), the tie adds

\[P_{bi} \mathrel{+}= \sum_a \lambda_{ai} \frac{\partial g_{ai}}{\partial \Delta U_{bi}}, \qquad P_{Ki} \mathrel{+}= \sum_a \lambda_{ai} \frac{\partial g_{ai}}{\partial \Delta U_{Ki}}, \qquad P_{\lambda_{ai}} \mathrel{+}= g_{ai},\]

and the tangent, symmetric as the tie stems from the potential \(\sum_{ai} \lambda_{ai} g_{ai}\). The finite element side receives the force \(-\int_\Gamma N_b \lambda_i \, d\Gamma\), the RKPM side the opposite one.

The integral. Gauss quadrature on every element edge, in the configuration at the start of the increment. The reproducing kernel shape functions at a quadrature point are those of the interface particle the point lies on. A field that is linear in space is represented exactly on both sides, so it is tied exactly, whatever the quadrature (the patch test).

Rules for the interface.

  • The finite element side carries the multipliers. Its nodes must not be prescribed by a Dirichlet boundary condition as well, which would constrain them twice.

  • The finite element side is given by its interface nodes, in order along the interface: corner node, midside node, corner node, midside node, …, corner node – the edges of quadratic elements. The tie checks that the order is one along the interface: every midside node lies near the middle of its corners (isQuadraticEdge()), and the interface does not turn back at a corner node (turnsBack()). A node set in the order of its labels, for instance, is refused.

  • The RKPM side is given by its particles along the interface.
