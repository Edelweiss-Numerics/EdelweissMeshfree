Models

Model are the core of the MPM simulation. They hold information about the domain (cells, material points), the materials, the sets, etc.

MPMModel class

class edelweissmeshfree.models.mpmmodel.MPMModel(dimension, axisymmetric=False)[source]

This is is a standard mpm model tree. It takes care of the correct number of variables, for nodes and scalar degrees of freedom, and it manages the fields.

Parameters:

  • axisymmetric (bool) – True if the model is the section r-z of a body of revolution, see axisymmetric; its particles must be axisymmetric ones, and it takes no material points, cells or cell elements, which integrate over the section, not over the rings (prepareYourself()).

_acceptElementStates()

Let every element accept its computed state, across the available threads.

An element’s acceptLastState touches only that element’s own state buffers – for a Marmot element it is the single self._stateVars[:] = self._stateVarsTemp copy – so the elements are independent of one another and this loop parallelizes without any coordination.

It is worth parallelizing because of how the explicit solver uses it, not because of how the implicit ones do. An implicit analysis calls this once per converged increment, amortised over a Newton loop and a linear solve, where 19 ms disappears into the noise. Explicit dynamics calls it on every one of millions of increments: on the anchor pry-out model it was 15.6 % of the whole step, the single largest cost outside the element kernels themselves, and every millisecond of it was serial Python holding 31 of 32 threads idle.

_resizeNodeFieldsForNodes(journal)

Resize the existing NodeFields in place for the current self.nodeSets["all"], instead of rebuilding nodeFields from scratch as _prepareVariablesAndFields() does. Used by mesh mutators (e.g. AMR’s hadaptivity._materialize) so that NodeField identity – and hence any NodeFieldSubset or reference a consumer cached – survives a topology change. ScalarVariables (e.g. Lagrange multipliers of constraints) are rebuilt, but values are preserved by name for constraints that still exist, so their converged state survives the refinement too.

Everything here is recomputed from the current elements, constraints and nodeSets["all"] – the node order of the resized fields is the model’s node creation order, which is why one call after several mutations yields the same layout as one call per mutation. Inside a deferring changes() window the call is therefore only noted, and made once when the window closes.

Parameters:

journal (Journal) – The journal instance.

advanceToTime(time)

Accept the current state of the model at the end of an increment, and set the new time: the state of its entities (acceptTheStateOfTheEntities()), then that of its constraints (acceptTheStateOfTheConstraints()).

Both accept the state of their last evaluation, which must be the one at the configuration accepted. That is what every solver evaluates last, except NED in a model with entities that carry their own inertia: there the constraints are evaluated at the accepted configuration only after the entities are reconnected, so NED calls the two parts itself, with the reconnection and the evaluation between them (see acceptIncrement()).

Parameters:

time (float) – The new time.

advanceVelocity(timeIncrement)

Advance the velocity of the model by its acceleration over a time increment, \(\boldsymbol{V} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{V} + \Delta t \, \boldsymbol{A}\), in every node field, on every node.

An explicit solver integrates the velocity staggered half an increment behind the solution within its step, and publishes the acceleration at the end of every increment. At the end of its step, it advances the velocity by the half increment it lags behind, so that the step leaves the velocity at its end time, as every solver does (see the documentation page Node field results). Where the acceleration is zero, the velocity stays as it is. The model does this itself, without a discrete system: a step resumed at its very end has built none.

Parameters:

timeIncrement (float) – The time increment.

updateConnectivity()[source]

Bring the connectivity up to date with the converged state, before an increment, and say what that changed; see updateConnectivity(). Called by EdelweissFE’s implicit solvers at the start of every increment and of every retry after a cutback, and by the explicit dynamic solver NED after every increment, on the kernel functions the particles reached, and when it resumes a step (see NED).

First the managers assign the material points to the cells they lie in (mpmManagers) and the particles to the kernel functions they reach (particleManagers), each from its converged state: a failed attempt may have left a trial state behind. Then the constraints follow, which couple the nodes the managers have just assigned. Finally, the test functions of the particles are corrected for the new connectivity (vciManagers).

A change of the active domain is a change of the connectivity, not of the mesh: no node is created or moved. If the nodes of the active domain are fixed (_activeNodesAreFixed), the numbering of the nodes stands, and the change is one of the locations: only the particles that changed and the constraints are located anew, which is what rebuilding would give, at a fraction of its cost. Otherwise the active domain may change, and with it the numbering.

Returns:

What changed: NONE if nothing did, LOCATIONS if the active nodes are fixed, else NUMBERING, or what the constraints changed, if that is more.

Return type:

Change

writeRestart(restartFile)[source]

Write the converged state of the model to a restart checkpoint: EdelweissFE’s model state (see writeRestart()), and the state of every material point and particle.

The material point method and RKPM are updated-Lagrangian: the material points and particles carry the state, and their positions follow from it. The connectivity – which cells hold which material points, which kernel functions reach which particles, where bonded kernel functions sit – follows from the positions, and is rebuilt by the first connectivity update of the resumed run (updateConnectivity()).

Parameters:

Additional information.

analyticalFields

AnalyticalFields in the model.

axisymmetric

the first coordinate is the radius r, the second the axial coordinate z, and every point of the section stands for the ring it sweeps about the axis. What the model integrates is then integrated over the rings, with the circumference \(2 \pi r\) – the axisymmetric elements their volumes, the contact points their areas (see outOfPlaneExtentAt()) –, so that the forces and the masses at the nodes are those of the full rings. Set by *job, domain=axisymmetric. class edelweissmeshfree.models.mpmdiscretesystem.CriticalTimeStepEstimateFromTheFieldMatrix(fieldMatrix, orderOfGroup, groupOfDof, rowSumsInTheOwnBlock, factorOfThePenaltyRows, collatzWielandtRows=None)[source]

The estimate of the critical time step of the particles, from the field matrix \(\boldsymbol{Q}\) of the largest row sums of their mass-scaled stiffness per block of fields (see MPMDiscreteSystem.criticalTimeStepOfEntitiesWithOwnInertia()), in the form the penalty constraints add to.

A penalty constraint acts on one field, and couples it with itself: its row sums \(r_i\) add to the particles’ row sums \(Z_{i g}\) within the field block \(g\) of the degree of freedom \(i\), so the diagonal entry of the field matrix becomes \(Q_{gg} = \max_{i \in g} (Z_{ig} + r_i)\), the other entries stay. Entry by entry, the absolute value of the particles’ and the constraints’ stiffness together is at most the sum of their absolute values, so the raised field matrix bounds it block by block, and its Perron root, which grows with its entries, bounds every eigenvalue – for an unsymmetric tangent as well (theory page on the critical time step of EdelweissFE, “The particles: the rows inside their field matrix”). Where the constraints raise no diagonal entry, and the weights are constant per field, the estimate is the particles’ own. This holds under one condition: every constraint couples degrees of freedom of one field of the particles only (a support, a contact or a tie of the displacement); a penalty that coupled two fields, e.g. the displacement with the nonlocal field, would add to an off-diagonal entry of the field matrix, which this form does not hold. This is assumed, not checked. A constraint that couples particles and finite elements at once is taken with the larger of the elements’ bound and this one; that this bounds the two together is not proven (same page, “What the estimate does not see”).

With carried weights (see MPMDiscreteSystem.criticalTimeStepOfEntitiesWithOwnInertia(), “Weights carried from estimate to estimate”), \(\boldsymbol{Q}\) is the field matrix of the weighted matrix \(\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}|\boldsymbol{S}|\boldsymbol{W}\), the row sums are those of its rows, and the critical time step of the blocks of each order is the larger of the one from \(\boldsymbol{Q}\) and the one from the rows of the Collatz-Wielandt bound with the weights scaled by the Perron vector of \(\boldsymbol{Q}\), to both of which the rows of the penalty constraints add. A constraint’s row \(r_i\) is the row of \(\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}|\boldsymbol{S}^\mathrm{c}|\boldsymbol{W}\) where the weights are constant on its field, and the particles keep them constant in every field a constraint acts on at the estimate. Elsewhere – a constraint that acts on another field since the estimate, e.g. a contact paired anew – the row is at most \(r_i \max_{j \in g} w_j / w_i\), which is added instead: a bound that holds, if loosely; where it lowers the critical time step below the one in use, the particles are estimated anew (isDue()), now with weights constant on that field.

Parameters:

  • fieldMatrix (ndarray) – The field matrix \(\boldsymbol{Q}\) of the particles.

  • orderOfGroup (list[str]) – Whether each block of fields is integrated by central differences (“second”) or by forward Euler (“first”).

  • groupOfDof (ndarray) – The block of fields of each degree of freedom of the numbering; -1 for one of no block.

  • rowSumsInTheOwnBlock (ndarray) – The row sum \(Z_{i g(i)} / w_i\) of each degree of freedom within its own block; zero for one of no block.

  • factorOfThePenaltyRows (ndarray) – The factor \(\max_{j \in g(i)} w_j / w_i\) of the row of a penalty constraint at each degree of freedom: one where the weights are constant on the block, as they are without carried weights.

  • collatzWielandtRows (ndarray | None) – The rows \((|\boldsymbol{S}|\boldsymbol{v})_i / v_i\) of the Collatz-Wielandt bound with the weights \(v_i = c_{g(i)} w_i\), \(\boldsymbol{c}\) the Perron vector of the field matrix of the order; None without carried weights.

The discrete system of a meshfree model, on its active domain: the background cells that hold material points, the nodes these cells and the particles’ kernel functions reach, and the finite elements with their nodes.

Two kinds of nodes take part. The nodes of finite elements (and of cell elements) carry their total solution from increment to increment, as in a finite element model. The nodes of the grid and of the kernel functions do not: the material point method and RKPM are incremental on the current configuration – the cells are reset after every increment, and the kernel functions move with their particles, while the state lives in the material points and particles. Their nodal values are the coefficients of one increment: every increment starts them at zero, so that their solution U is zero, and their increment dU is their meaningful nodal value.

The model’s node fields are read and written straight, by node: read on the nodes that carry an entry (see readEntryOfNodeField()), written on the nodes of the active domain from the vectors of the discrete system, zero on all other nodes (see writeEntryOfNodeField()).

Parameters:

  • locations (MPMDofLocations) – The locations of the entities in the numbering of the degrees of freedom of the active domain; the finite elements, the active cells (those that hold material points, sorted by their number), the cell elements and the particles, in the order in which the solver computes them, are the elements of the discrete system.

  • nodesWithVolatileFieldValues (NodeSet) – The nodes of the active cells and of the particles’ kernel functions.

  • modelForLinearSolver – The model reduced to its active domain, for the linear solver.

criticalTimeStepOfEntitiesWithOwnInertia(lumpedMass, lumpedDamping, forEachChunk, weightsOfTheEstimate=None)[source]

An estimate of the critical time step of NED’s explicit scheme for the particles, a lower bound of it, from their stiffness at the current state and the assembled lumped mass and damping; NED decides when it is taken anew (CriticalTimeStepOfEntitiesWithOwnInertia). No stiffness is assembled: every particle bounds the row sums of the absolute value of its own (addBoundOfAbsoluteStiffnessTimes), at a cost proportional to its nodes.

It is a property of the ASSEMBLED system: a particle’s own bound, \(\lambda_\mathrm{max}(\boldsymbol{M}_p^{-1} \boldsymbol{K}_p)\) as for a finite element, is unbounded, because a particle’s stiffness couples nodes at which its own lumped mass vanishes (the mass is taken at its centre, its smoothed gradients at its faces).

What is bounded. The degrees of freedom with an inertia are integrated by central differences: \(\boldsymbol{M}\ddot{\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{C}\dot{\boldsymbol{u}} + \boldsymbol{K} \boldsymbol{u} = \boldsymbol{0}\) is stable for \(\Delta t \le 2/\omega_\mathrm{max}\), with \(\omega_\mathrm{max}^2\) the largest eigenvalue of \(\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{K}\). The damping does not lower this limit, since NED takes it with the mean of the velocities before and after the update (a damping evaluated with the old velocity alone would). The degrees of freedom without inertia but with a damping (a first-order nonlocal field) are integrated by forward Euler, stable for \(\Delta t \le 2/\lambda_\mathrm{max}(\boldsymbol{C}^{-1}\boldsymbol{K})\). The coupling between the two sets is not included.

Why the absolute value of the unsymmetric tangent. The tangent of gradient-enhanced particles is unsymmetric, and its coupling blocks differ by orders of magnitude once scaled by the masses: the stress depends on the nonlocal field as strongly as on the strain (on a boundary of GM7, through the over-nonlocal strain), the nonlocal field depends on the strain with a factor of one, and the micro-inertia of the nonlocal field is far below the mass. An eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of the coupled system sees the geometric mean of the two coupling blocks, the symmetric part of the tangent their arithmetic mean: a bound of the symmetric part follows the large block alone and falls far below the critical time step whenever a particle loads a boundary. Instead, every eigenvalue, real or complex, satisfies \(|\lambda| \le \rho(|\boldsymbol{S}|)\), the spectral radius of the absolute value of \(\boldsymbol{S} = \boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{K}\boldsymbol{D}\) (similar to \(\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{K}\), with \(\boldsymbol{D} = \boldsymbol{M}^{-1/2}\), or \(\boldsymbol{C}^{-1/2}\) on the first-order set). \(|\boldsymbol{K}|\) is accumulated particle by particle, which bounds the absolute value of the sum.

The bound of \(\rho(|\boldsymbol{S}|)\) from one pass over the particles. The Collatz-Wielandt bound \(\rho(|\boldsymbol{S}|) \le \max_i (|\boldsymbol{S}|\boldsymbol{w})_i / w_i\) holds for every positive \(\boldsymbol{w}\); the weights are taken constant on each field: \(\boldsymbol{w} = \sum_f c_f \boldsymbol{1}_f\). The particles give upper bounds \(\hat{Z}_{if} \ge Z_{if}\) of the row sums of \(|\boldsymbol{S}|\) over the columns of each field, \(Z_{if} = \sum_{j \in f} |S_{ij}|\) (each particle its share, addBoundOfAbsoluteStiffnessTimes with the weights \(\boldsymbol{D}\) on the columns of the field), and with them the field matrix \(Q_{gf} = \max_{i \in g} \hat{Z}_{if}\), the largest row sum of the block of fields \(g, f\). With \(\boldsymbol{c}\) the Perron vector of \(\boldsymbol{Q}\), every row \(i\) of field \(g\) gives \((|\boldsymbol{S}|\boldsymbol{w})_i \le (\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{c})_g = \rho(\boldsymbol{Q}) w_i\), so \(\rho(|\boldsymbol{S}|) \le \rho(\boldsymbol{Q})\): the Gershgorin bound per block of fields, coupled by the largest eigenvalue of the small field matrix, which takes the geometric mean of the coupling blocks. With a single field it is the Gershgorin bound.

Weights carried from estimate to estimate (weightsOfTheEstimate, NED’s carry-critical-time-step-weights). The weights constant per field are the start of the power iteration towards the Perron vector of \(|\boldsymbol{S}|\), where the Collatz-Wielandt bound is exact. Weights \(\boldsymbol{w}\) carried from the last estimate take the place of the ones in the same pass: the columns of field \(f\) are the weights on its degrees of freedom, which gives \(\hat{Z}_{if} \ge \sum_{j \in f} |S_{ij}| w_j\), and \(Q_{gf} = \max_{i \in g} \hat{Z}_{if} / w_i\) is the field matrix of \(\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}|\boldsymbol{S}|\boldsymbol{W}\), which has the eigenvalues of \(|\boldsymbol{S}|\), so again \(\rho(|\boldsymbol{S}|) \le \rho(\boldsymbol{Q})\). The pass costs as much as without carried weights: the same columns, other values. With \(\boldsymbol{c}\) the Perron vector of \(\boldsymbol{Q}\), the weights \(v_i = c_g w_i\) of the rows of field \(g\) give \(\hat{\boldsymbol{a}} = \sum_f c_f \hat{\boldsymbol{Z}}_f \ge |\boldsymbol{S}|\boldsymbol{v}\) and the Collatz-Wielandt bound \(\max_i \hat{a}_i / v_i\), which is never above \(\rho(\boldsymbol{Q})\), since every row gives \(\hat{a}_i / v_i \le (\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{c})_g / c_g\); the smaller of the two is taken, against the round-off of the Perron vector. Both hold for every positive \(\boldsymbol{w}\), whatever state it was refined at: the weights carried need not fit the stiffness of now to bound it, only to bound it tightly. At the start, with the weights constant per field, the estimate is never below the one without carried weights. The weights for the next estimate are \(\hat{\boldsymbol{a}}\), scaled to a largest weight of one per scheme: one step of the power iteration per estimate, which tightens the estimate towards \(\rho(|\boldsymbol{S}|)\) over a few estimates as long as the stiffness changes slowly from estimate to estimate.

Where the stiffness changes faster than the weights follow, an estimate with carried weights can be below the one with weights constant per field. NED starts the weights anew (all zero) whenever an estimate with them is below the estimate they started from (CriticalTimeStepOfEntitiesWithOwnInertia), so that an increment that is lowered only never falls below the smallest estimate with weights constant per field of the step (followed, it can fall below the one of the same configuration, down to the one the weights started from). Taking the smaller of the two estimates in every estimate instead would take the columns with weights constant per field as well, which cost about as much again as those of the carried weights. A degree of freedom without a weight – one that has no inertia or damping at the last estimate – starts at the largest weight of its block of fields, the weight every degree of freedom of the block has at the start (_weightsOfThisEstimate()). So does every degree of freedom of a field a constraint of the discrete system acts on: there the weights stay constant per field, so that the rows of the penalty constraints add as they are (CriticalTimeStepEstimateFromTheFieldMatrix).

The power iteration shrinks the weights of the degrees of freedom far from the stiffest ones by a factor per estimate, without limit: within a few dozen estimates they fall below what a double resolves next to the largest weight, and a degree of freedom whose stiffness then changes, e.g. as a particle reaches another kernel function, is weighted by round-off, which raises the bound far above the one without weights until the iteration has grown the weight back. So no weight is kept below the machine epsilon of the largest one (_weightsForTheNextEstimate()): a weight below it adds nothing to a sum with the largest, so the limit removes only what the arithmetic cannot carry anyway, and every degree of freedom stays weighted, so that a change of its stiffness is followed within one estimate. Any larger limit (a hundredth of the largest weight, say) bounds the particles of a running analysis as tightly, the machine epsilon is the smallest that can be kept.

The row sums without the stiffness. A particle’s stiffness has as many blocks as the square of its nodes, but it sees the nodal increments only through the increment it interpolates at its evaluation point (value, gradient and, if stabilized, second gradient): the rows of node \(A\) are linear in the shape functions of the other node \(B\), with factors of node \(A\) alone. Marmot sums \(w_B |K_{Ai,Bk}|\) over \(B\) from a few weighted moments of the shape functions, exactly for the value and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality over the nodes for the gradients, which keeps their directions (2/sqrt(3) of the exact sum for gradients spread evenly in 3D, exact for parallel ones). Every inequality bounds from above, so the estimate stays a lower bound of the critical time step.

The result is scaled by STABLE_FRACTION_OF_THE_TANGENT_LIMIT, since the explicit scheme of the particles is not exactly integrated with their tangent: their NSNI stabilization acts on the increment.

It is returned with the field matrix and the row sums of every degree of freedom in its own block, to which the row sums of penalty constraints add (CriticalTimeStepEstimateFromTheFieldMatrix).

  • forEachChunk (Callable[[list, Callable], None]) – The solver’s loop over chunks of independent entities.

edelweissmeshfree.models.mpmdiscretesystem.COEFFICIENT_AT_ROUND_OFF = 1e-12

A lumped coefficient of a node (a mass, a damping) below this fraction of the largest one of its field is zero up to round-off: the particles reach its kernel function only where it vanishes. A solver that divides by it would give the node an acceleration of round-off over round-off.

edelweissmeshfree.models.mpmdiscretesystem.STABLE_FRACTION_OF_THE_TANGENT_LIMIT = 0.85

The fraction of the critical time step of the particles’ tangent that the explicit scheme of the particles is stable for. Their NSNI stabilization acts on the increment of the step, not on the total displacement, so the scheme is not exactly the central difference method with their tangent: measured on 2D and 3D blocks of particles (support factors 1.6 to 3.4, quadratic and cubic kernels), it was stable up to 0.875 to 0.98 of the tangent’s limit.
