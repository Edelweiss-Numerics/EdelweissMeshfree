Models

Model are the core of the MPM simulation. They hold information about the domain (cells, material points), the materials, the sets, etc.

_acceptElementStates()

Let every element accept its computed state, across the available threads.

An element’s acceptLastState touches only that element’s own state buffers – for a Marmot element it is the single self._stateVars[:] = self._stateVarsTemp copy – so the elements are independent of one another and this loop parallelizes without any coordination.

It is worth parallelizing because of how the explicit solver uses it, not because of how the implicit ones do. An implicit analysis calls this once per converged increment, amortised over a Newton loop and a linear solve, where 19 ms disappears into the noise. Explicit dynamics calls it on every one of millions of increments: on the anchor pry-out model it was 15.6 % of the whole step, the single largest cost outside the element kernels themselves, and every millisecond of it was serial Python holding 31 of 32 threads idle.

_resizeNodeFieldsForNodes(journal)

Resize the existing NodeFields in place for the current self.nodeSets["all"], instead of rebuilding nodeFields from scratch as _prepareVariablesAndFields() does. Used by mesh mutators (e.g. AMR’s hadaptivity._materialize) so that NodeField identity – and hence any NodeFieldSubset or reference a consumer cached – survives a topology change. ScalarVariables (e.g. Lagrange multipliers of constraints) are rebuilt, but values are preserved by name for constraints that still exist, so their converged state survives the refinement too.

Everything here is recomputed from the current elements, constraints and nodeSets["all"] – the node order of the resized fields is the model’s node creation order, which is why one call after several mutations yields the same layout as one call per mutation. Inside a deferring changes() window the call is therefore only noted, and made once when the window closes.

Parameters:

journal (Journal) – The journal instance.

advanceVelocity(timeIncrement)

Advance the velocity of the model by its acceleration over a time increment, \(\boldsymbol{V} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{V} + \Delta t \, \boldsymbol{A}\), in every node field, on every node.

An explicit solver integrates the velocity staggered half an increment behind the solution within its step, and publishes the acceleration at the end of every increment. At the end of its step, it advances the velocity by the half increment it lags behind, so that the step leaves the velocity at its end time, as every solver does (see the documentation page Node field results). Where the acceleration is zero, the velocity stays as it is. The model does this itself, without a discrete system: a step resumed at its very end has built none.

Parameters:

timeIncrement (float) – The time increment.

setNewmarkParametersOfEntities(beta, gamma)[source]

Give the particles the Newmark parameters of the solver, at the start of every step of an implicit solver; see setNewmarkParametersOfEntities(). Each particle integrates the Newmark scheme at its own evaluation points, with its velocity and acceleration in its state; with \(\beta = \gamma = 0\), which every quasi-static solver passes, it carries no inertia. A particle without the properties newmark-beta beta and newmark-beta gamma has no inertia to integrate: it is accepted by a quasi-static solver, and refused by a dynamic one.

Parameters:

  • beta (float) – The Newmark parameter \(\beta\); 0 for no inertia.

  • gamma (float) – The Newmark parameter \(\gamma\); 0 for no inertia.

Raises:

ValueError – If a particle without Newmark parameters is to integrate its inertia.

updateConnectivity()[source]

Bring the connectivity up to date with the converged state, before an increment, and say what that changed; see updateConnectivity(). Called by EdelweissFE’s solvers at the start of every increment and of every retry after a cutback.

First the managers assign the material points to the cells they lie in (mpmManagers) and the particles to the kernel functions they reach (particleManagers), each from its converged state: a failed attempt may have left a trial state behind. Then the constraints follow, which couple the nodes the managers have just assigned. Finally, the test functions of the particles are corrected for the new connectivity (vciManagers).

A change of the active domain is a change of the connectivity, not of the mesh: no node is created or moved. If the nodes of the active domain are fixed (_activeNodesAreFixed), the numbering of the nodes stands, and the change is one of the locations: only the particles that changed and the constraints are located anew, which is what rebuilding would give, at a fraction of its cost. Otherwise the active domain may change, and with it the numbering.

Returns:

What changed: NONE if nothing did, LOCATIONS if the active nodes are fixed, else NUMBERING, or what the constraints changed, if that is more.

Return type:

Change

writeRestart(restartFile)[source]

Write the converged state of the model to a restart checkpoint: EdelweissFE’s model state (see writeRestart()), and the state of every material point and particle.

The material point method and RKPM are updated-Lagrangian: the material points and particles carry the state, and their positions follow from it. The connectivity – which cells hold which material points, which kernel functions reach which particles, where bonded kernel functions sit – follows from the positions, and is rebuilt by the first connectivity update of the resumed run (updateConnectivity()), as before every increment.

Parameters:

restartFile (File) – An open, writable h5py.File (or group) to write the checkpoint into.

MPMDiscreteSystem class

What an MPMModel answers EdelweissFE’s implicit solvers (NIST, NISTParallel, NISTPArcLength) when they (re)build their discrete system: the active domain, its degrees of freedom and the entities computed like elements, how the solution passes between the model and the solver’s vectors, and the update of the material points before the cells are computed. See EdelweissFE’s documentation, “What the solver asks the model”.

What the discrete system of a material point or RKPM model is made of: the answer an MPMModel gives EdelweissFE’s implicit solvers and MeshfreeExplicitDynamic whenever they (re)build their discrete system, see buildDiscreteSystem().

class edelweissmeshfree.models.mpmdiscretesystem.MPMDiscreteSystem(model, locations, nodesWithPersistentFieldValues, nodesWithVolatileFieldValues, modelForLinearSolver)[source]

The discrete system of a meshfree model, on its active domain: the background cells that hold material points, the nodes these cells and the particles’ kernel functions reach, and the finite elements with their nodes.

Two kinds of nodes take part. The nodes of finite elements (and of cell elements) carry their total solution from increment to increment, as in a finite element model. The nodes of the grid and of the kernel functions do not: the material point method and RKPM are incremental on the current configuration – the cells are reset after every increment, and the kernel functions move with their particles, while the state lives in the material points and particles. Their nodal values are the coefficients of one increment: every increment starts them at zero, so that their solution U is zero, and their increment dU is their meaningful nodal value.

The model’s node fields are read and written straight, by node: read on the nodes that carry an entry (see readEntryOfNodeField()), written on the nodes of the active domain from the vectors of the discrete system, zero on all other nodes (see writeEntryOfNodeField()).

Parameters:

  • model – The model.

  • locations (MPMDofLocations) – The locations of the entities in the numbering of the degrees of freedom of the active domain; the finite elements, the active cells (those that hold material points, sorted by their number), the cell elements and the particles, in the order in which the solver computes them, are the elements of the discrete system.

  • nodesWithPersistentFieldValues (NodeSet) – The nodes that carry their total solution from increment to increment: those of the finite elements and of the cell elements.

  • nodesWithVolatileFieldValues (NodeSet) – The nodes of the active cells and of the particles’ kernel functions.

  • modelForLinearSolver – The model reduced to its active domain, for the linear solver.

assembleLumpedMassOfEntitiesWithOwnInertia(forEachChunk)[source]

The lumped mass of the particles at the nodes of their current kernel functions, \(m_A = \sum_p \rho_0\, V_{0,p}\, T_A(\boldsymbol{x}_p)\), as each particle reports it (computeLumpedInertia), summed over the particles; see assembleLumpedMassOfEntitiesWithOwnInertia().
