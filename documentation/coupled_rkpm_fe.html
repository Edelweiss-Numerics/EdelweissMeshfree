Coupled RKPM + FE

An MPMModel may hold finite elements next to its particles: e.g. a structure of finite elements with an RKPM domain where the material is expected to deform strongly. Both are solved together, as one equation system, by EdelweissFE’s implicit static solvers (see Choosing a solver). The time integration is quasi-static.

Two kinds of nodes in one equation system

The model numbers the nodes of the finite elements and the nodes of the kernel functions in one equation system, the elements first, then the particles (buildEquationSystem()). The two kinds of nodes hold different things:

  • the nodes of the finite elements carry their total displacement from increment to increment, as in any finite element model;

  • the nodes of the kernel functions carry the coefficients of one increment: RKPM is updated-Lagrangian, the kernel functions move with their particles, and the state lives in the particles. Every increment starts them at zero.

MPMEquationSystemLayout passes the solution between the model and the solver’s vectors accordingly. At acceptance, it writes the solution U, the increment dU and the reaction P straight to the model’s node fields, by node: U is the total displacement at the nodes of the finite elements and zero at the nodes of the kernel functions, whose meaningful value is dU; every node outside the active domain holds zero (see EdelweissFE’s “Node field results” for the meaning of these entries).

The tie between the two domains

The two domains do not share nodes. Sharing a node would not tie them: the reproducing kernel shape functions are not interpolating, so the value at a kernel node is not the displacement there, while an element interprets the same value as the displacement of its node. Instead, a constraint ties the two displacement fields along the interface, ParticleFiniteElementMortarTie:

  • in the weak (mortar) sense: the difference of the displacements of the two sides, weighted by the shape functions of the element edges, integrates to zero. A displacement field that both sides represent exactly – a linear one – is tied exactly;

  • with Lagrange multipliers: one per interface node and component, the interface traction, interpolated like the displacement of the element edges. Unlike a penalty factor, they leave no gap and need no parameter;

  • incrementally, on the current configuration: the tie couples the increments of both sides, on the configuration at the start of the increment, where RKPM defines its shape functions. The integrals are computed anew at the start of every increment, in updateConnectivity, and the tie is linear during the increment.

The finite element side of the interface consists of the edges of quadratic elements, given by their nodes in order: corner, midside, corner, … The tie checks the order on the geometry: every midside node must lie near the middle of its corners, and the interface must not turn back at a corner node. A node set in the order of its labels, with the corner nodes numbered before the midside nodes, is refused. The RKPM side is given by the particles along the interface. The interface nodes of the finite elements carry the multipliers; they must not carry a Dirichlet boundary condition too.

from edelweissmeshfree.constraints.particlefiniteelementmortartie import ParticleFiniteElementMortarTie

model.constraints["tie"] = ParticleFiniteElementMortarTie(
    "tie", model, model.particleSets["rkpm_left"], model.nodeSets["fe_right"]
)

The constraint must be part of the model before model.prepareYourself, which creates the multipliers.

Building a coupled model

The finite elements come from EdelweissFE’s generators, inside a topology change; the kernel functions get node labels after those of the elements; the sections of the elements are assigned by model.prepareYourself:

with model.topology.changes():
    PlaneRectQuadGenerator(
        "fe", model, journal,
        configuration=PlaneRectQuadSchema(x0=0.0, length=3.0, h=1.0, nX=6, nY=2, elType="CPE8UL"),
    )
model.sections["fe"] = PlaneSection(
    "fe", model, {"name": "CompressibleNeoHooke", "properties": materialProperties},
    [model.elementSets["fe_all"]], configuration=PlaneSectionSchema(thickness=1.0),
)

generateRectangularKernelFunctionGrid(model, journal, kernelFunctionFactory, ...,
                                      firstKernelFunctionNumber=max(model.nodes) + 1)

Dirichlet boundary conditions on finite element nodes are EdelweissFE’s dirichlet step actions, as in a finite element model.

The example

examples/170_coupled_rkpm_fe_cantilever_test is a cantilever whose middle part is an RKPM domain between two parts of quadratic finite strain elements, clamped and deflected at finite element nodes, and solved by NISTParallel. Its independent check is the same cantilever made of finite elements only: the coupled model reproduces its tip reaction and its deflection line, and both approach the same limit as the elements and particles are refined.
